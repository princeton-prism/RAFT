Problem Statement

Generalist vision-language-action (VLA) policies still need post-training to reach high success on a new target task. With only a few demonstrations, plain fine-tuning improves success in the demonstration setup but makes the policy brittle: on a physical DROID robot it succeeds in 60% of in-distribution trials but only about 28% once the object color, distractors or background change.

TL;DR: Train a reasoning adapter first, freeze it, then fine-tune on actions. VLAs post-trained this way stay robust to new objects, clutter, poses and backgrounds that break plain fine-tuning.

Method

RAFT is a two-stage post-training procedure. Stage I trains a low-rank adapter (LoRA) on task-relevant reasoning annotations, such as object bounding boxes, keypoints and gripper traces, while the rest of the policy stays frozen. Stage II freezes that adapter and fine-tunes all remaining policy parameters on the action demonstrations.

Results

Across two real robots and two simulated tasks, RAFT has the highest out-of-distribution success while matching or exceeding the baselines in distribution.

Setting

Ablations

Staging and a frozen adapter matter

On simulated pick and place, co-training reasoning and actions jointly, or leaving the adapter trainable in Stage II, both fall below RAFT.

Split

Broader reasoning data helps downstream

Adding reasoning data from other scenes to Stage I lowers held-out reasoning loss and improves downstream success at every demonstration budget.

Task

RAFT is compatible with RETAIN weight merging

On Franka open drawer, using RAFT instead of plain fine-tuning as RETAIN's fine-tuned endpoint raises pooled success from 26.9% to 32.8% (merging at α = 0.9, 106 trials per condition).

Representation Analysis

RAFT attends more to the annotated task regions: at layer 11 they receive 3.2–3.4 times their share of the image, versus 1.9–2.1 for plain fine-tuning. Its late layers also keep more object information: linear probes on layers 14–17 decode object identity with 0.51–0.53 balanced accuracy, versus 0.45.
